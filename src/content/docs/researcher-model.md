MeaningWire separates **what data means** from the systems that happen to store or transport it.

## Semantic direction

The project is developing vendor-independent canonical semantics, explicit mappings, provenance, and authority boundaries. Systems map at the edges rather than requiring every pair of systems to maintain a bespoke point-to-point contract.

Mapping relationships are explicit: `exact`, `equivalent`, `broader`, `narrower`, `derived`, `transformed`, `lossy`, and `unsupported`. Loss and unsupported semantics are represented rather than hidden.

## Standards context

The research direction draws from public standards and semantic models including OAGIS, Schema.org, OASIS UBL, GS1 EPCIS/CBV, FIBO, JSON Schema, OpenAPI, AsyncAPI, CloudEvents, and JSON-LD. These references inform interoperability work; their use does not imply endorsement or formal affiliation.

## Provenance before inference

MeaningWire treats provenance as first-class evidence. AI or other non-human inference may assist analysis or transformation, but inference does not become human approval merely because it is recorded in a canonical envelope.

## Maturity matters

Research and experimental work should retain explicit maturity. MeaningWire Labs uses a progression from discovery and research through experimental/preview/stable states, with rejected, superseded, and deprecated outcomes preserved when useful.

## Evidence over claims

Prefer a reproducible fixture, schema, mapping, validation run, or compatibility result over an unsupported compatibility statement. The current public baseline is deliberately small so its claims can remain inspectable.
